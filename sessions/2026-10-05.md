# 2026-10-05 — Node container, session grants, and the sharing regime

Planned and registered the node build-out: a deployable multi-arch peer, the
grant/trust model, the Open WebUI surface, conversation passing, `MASTERSPIKE`,
and the inter-node sharing regime. Root `PLAN.md` Steps 6–7, `peer-node/PLAN.md`
Steps 2–8, `master-node` Steps 10–12.

## The error worth remembering

I reported, as a confirmed bug, that the vendored `vault` agent had never
loaded because it sat in `.opencode/agent/` (singular) while the docs name
`.opencode/agents/`. It was false. The agent had loaded the entire time.

The cause was my own check: `opencode agent list | grep …`. That listing is ~9 MB
of per-agent permission config, and a non-seekable stdout loses the tail —
4.5 MB and four agents through a pipe, 9.4 MB and nine through a file. The two
entries that vanished were `title` and `vault`, the *last* two, which reads
exactly like "the agent is missing". A cold cache plus a file redirect against
the original directory state showed `vault (primary)` loading fine, and that
opencode accepts either spelling.

Two lessons are now structural rather than remembered: read large tool output
through a file, and never let a DoD assert something not directly observed. Four
standing verification rules are recorded in the root `PLAN.md`.

## Measured, not assumed

Probing found `ports.json` claiming four listeners that do not exist: the
dispatch bridge is 110 lines with no bind code, and each `agents/*/agent.py` is
a two-line `print`. `51820` claims implemented but has no `wg` here. The boolean
`implemented` cannot distinguish "no listener" from "provisioning exists", which
is why root Step 6 replaces it with an explicit state.

## The `_staging` defect

Verifying peer Step 1's relocated-copy DoD surfaced a real bug: `.gitignore`
ignored `_staging/` outright while the root charter documents it as a category
and `test_layout.py` requires a tracked `.gitkeep`. A fresh clone therefore had
no `_staging/` and `test_vault_category_dir_survives_a_fresh_clone[_staging]`
failed there — the local working tree hid it. Fixed by tracking the root
directory through its `.gitkeep` with staged pages still ignored, and keeping the
two role subvault queues ignored, since neither role charter documents them.

## Decisions taken

- **Master**: peer side now, offline operator signing interim. The approval
  service is not built, so nothing may depend on a live Master for a peer's
  correctness — the peer enforces its own deadlines.
- **Trust**: two tiers. Config stays Master-signed per file; knowledge is
  grant-scoped with provenance, a hash-chained ledger and `_raw/` quarantine. A
  signature per page would be unusable at conversational rate.
- **Subagents**: full grant inheritance, as chosen. Mitigated by recording the
  acting child session in the ledger, so authority traces to the parent and
  audit traces to the child.
- **Isolation**: severs the fabric data plane, keeps the Master evidence
  channel. A severed Master link would leave the required review unsatisfiable.
- **Transport**: time-boxed data-plane stanza, never a widening of the authority
  plane — `overlay.json:14` requires exactly one `AllowedIPs` address and
  `security-policies.json:51` restricts egress to the Master.
- **Container at peer Step 2**, right after contracts: most verifiable phase on
  this host, and it forces the port/state/persistence questions early.
- **Passing**: intra-node uses only native primitives (same session plus
  `agent`, `fork`, `children`). The earlier conversation-hash identity is removed
  by the grant instead of being patched.

## Sharing regime, as specified

Invitations live 1 h; a share closes after 20 min idle; the Master has 2 h to
ratify; non-ratification severs the fabric plane while peer-to-peer continues,
and the share ends at 10 h. Both nodes drop to `unauthenticated` and `flagged`;
ratification authorises with the flag **retained for audit**. Two nodes are
symmetric with no Master interposed and verify shared information against the
existing knowledge base; three or more bring the Master in, and a third node
joining mid-share re-evaluates it rather than grandfathering it. All four clocks
are clock-injected so their tests run in seconds.

## Verified

`make test-all` (185 peer tests), `make validate`, `make lint`, a fresh peer
bundle and its extracted copy resolving all 9 agents, and a relocated parent tree
passing the fabric and peer suites. Peer Step 1 closed on that evidence.

## Not verified

Cross-node behaviour, everything Master-side, GPU inference, and live WireGuard.
This host has one peer, no master, no second node, no `nvidia-smi`, `wg` or
`nft`. Those DoDs are `confirm: user`, not silent passes.

## Master node Steps 15–16: the unbuilt boundary inverted

**What landed.** `master-node/` gained `services/`, `scripts/`, `systemd/`,
`manifests/`, `blockchain/` and `security/`. The peer's services are loaded **by
path** (`services/sparknet-parity.py`) with a hard allowlist and a loud failure
when the peer is unreachable — no fallback to a local copy. Two systemd units, a
hash-chained ledger that verifies, and a keygen producing three named keys.

**The correction that matters.** Earlier today I recorded a decision to put the
Master at `/mnt/knowledge/master-node`, outside the repo, reasoning that the
unbuilt-boundary test would then stay true. That was wrong twice over. The test
existed to stop a role's *prose* drifting from its code; satisfying it by moving
the code elsewhere would have kept a false claim green forever. The right move is
the one the test's own module docstring predicted: invert the assertion in the same
change that adds code, and update `AGENTS.md`, `MISSION.md`,
`docs/master-control-plane.md`, the root `AGENTS.md`, `README.md` and `index.md`
alongside. Two assertions were carried over untouched — no key material in the
vault, and no live `wg0.conf` — because they were never about whether the role is
built.

**A Master is a peer.** This forced a real change at the fabric level. The old
`test_each_role_declares_its_own_role_value` asserted `master-node/` must declare
`role: master`, encoding the idea that the Master is a separate species. Under the
elected model a node is a peer until three distinct members sign a trust root, so
`node-identity.json` says `role: peer`, is election-eligible, and records
`operational_key.held: false`. The replacement tests are stronger: they assert no
deployment site may claim `role: master`, and that no template may assert
`"elected": true`.

**Three bugs found, all of them mine.**

1. `shared/sparknet-consensus.py` loaded `sparknet-authz` and then imported
   `cryptography` anyway, because authz had no private-key-from-base64
   primitive. Ruff caught it as an unused import. Fixed by adding
   `private_key_from_b64`, `sign_bytes` and `generate_keypair` to authz, so
   key parsing has one home. `attach_root_signatures` had the same problem, and
   was also handing one process all three private keys — now documented as a
   fixture, with `sign_root` + `merge_root_signatures` as the real ceremony.
2. `sparknet-master-keygen.sh` called `generate-keypair` twice per key, so the
   public half belonged to a *different* keypair than the private half. Both
   halves loaded, both were 32 bytes, and nothing would ever verify. Caught by a
   test that requires each public half to correspond to its private half;
   mutation M7 confirms it fails when the two-call form returns.
3. `make lint` was a false green over the peer role. `peer-node/pyproject.toml`
   has `[tool.ruff]` but no `[tool.ruff.lint] select`, so the peer was graded
   against ruff's 413-rule default while the root used a deliberate 108 — and the
   peer's own lint target looked for ruff only in `peer-node/.venv` and reported a
   skip. Aligned the rule set, and made an unrunnable lint gate fail rather than
   skip. The peer's 18 apparent findings were all artifacts of the bad config.

**Two DoD items were unsatisfiable and are recorded as amended**, not quietly
satisfied. Step 16(3) said the master scripts should delegate to the peer's; they
cannot, because `sparknet-env.sh` hardcodes `SPARKNET_ROLE="peer"` and would
resolve this role to the peer's vault — exactly the silent misdirection the
bundler's `.env` exclusion exists to prevent. Step 16(4) wanted a signer unit and
an election timer; a unit whose `ExecStart` names a service that does not exist is
precisely what the new wiring test was written to catch. Both land with their
implementation (17 and 14).

**Verification.** `make test-all`, `lint`, `validate`, `contract-test` all pass. A
master bundle builds, extracts elsewhere, and proves its parity resolution landed
*inside the extracted tree* rather than on the build host — the anti-false-green
check the peer's `.env` exclusion already forced. Seven mutations (M1–M7) each
land and each fail the intended test. Steps 13/14 had complete Status lines but
unticked checkboxes, so `make status` claimed 2 of 17.

**Not verified, and not claimed.** No live cluster. No election, no signed
registry entry, no key across a process boundary, no containment test. The signer
is Step 17 and the cursor is now there.

## Step 17 — the isolated signer

Cursor moved 17 -> 18. The signer is the one thing this role must never get
wrong, so most of the session went into making its refusals provable rather than
its happy path work.

**Five tests failed on first run and every failure was mine, not the code's.**
Worth writing down, because the pattern recurred: each was a test that encoded a
wrong belief about the API and reported it as a security failure.

| Failed | Reality |
|---|---|
| happy path | `authz.evaluate` takes an `Ed25519PublicKey` **object**, and the registry is `{"authorizations": [entry]}`, not a dict keyed by fingerprint. Both report `malformed_record` |
| forged root | the fixture returned an already-**signed** root, so re-signing it with attacker keys tripped `merge_root_signatures`' own equivocation refusal and hid the behaviour under test |
| stale root | my test asserted nothing (`if False else`) |
| TCP socket | `Path("tcp://h:p")` collapses to `tcp:/h:p`, so the scheme check could not see the input it exists to catch — **a real defect in the signer**, not just the test |
| unit wiring | the assertion scanned whole unit files and flagged a *description* mentioning the signer; it should only inspect `ExecStart` |

**The socket fix is the one that mattered.** The scheme check is now run against
the raw argument string, and argparse is deliberately not given `type=Path` for
that argument. A check that silently cannot see its own input is worse than no
check, because it reads as coverage.

**Stale roots: the ledger is the memory, not root validity.** A superseded root
is *valid* on its own — it names this node, it names this key, all three members
signed it. So there is no way to detect a revert by asking whether a root is
well-formed, and my first attempt asserted a contradiction rather than a property.
Every signature now records the `root_id` it was issued under; a ledger naming a
different root means this process is being pointed somewhere it has already left,
and starting is refused without `--accept-requalification`. Adopting a *newer*
root needs the same flag, which is right — that is the moment to be deliberate
about. A plain restart under the same root is not a revert, and that boundary is
pinned by its own test, because a blunt check that refused it would make the
signer impossible to restart.

**A defect the tests found in the signer itself.** An entry can be filed under a
fingerprint that is not the hash of its identity; `authz.evaluate` rejects that as
`unknown_fingerprint`, so signing it produces a perfectly valid signature over an
authorization *no peer will ever accept*. The operator sees `ok` and the grant
silently does nothing. Found only because the fixture built a real entry instead
of a plausible one.

**The build caught a real ordering dependency.** The moment the signer file
existed, `make test-all` failed: `sparknet-signer.py is not referenced by any
systemd unit or Makefile target, so nothing would ever run it`. That is the
"nothing inert" rule doing its job — and it is why the unit was written only now,
after the service passed 36 tests, rather than alongside a stub.

**The unit is excluded from `make units` on purpose.** Installing a role and
enabling a process that holds the Master signing key are different acts; the
second belongs to the election. `make signer-unit` stages it without enabling.
`--node-id` and `--root` have no `:-` defaults either: systemd expands an unset
variable to empty, and the process must refuse rather than start as the wrong
node.

**Mutations M8–M12 all caught, and one lied.** M10's replacement text did not
match the branch it targeted, so the file was unchanged, the test passed, and the
mutation was reported as "survived". Reapplied correctly, it fails the test. A
mutation check that never applied is the easiest kind of false all-clear to
accept, which is why it is in `log.md` and the plan rather than quietly redone.

**Also fixed, found while verifying rather than planned:** the master identity
manifest had drifted to `wireguard_pubkey_b64` while the contract, the peer
manifest, `authz` and the docs all said `wireguard_public_key_b64` — and because
`fingerprint()` ignores fields outside `IDENTITY_FIELDS`, this raised nothing and
warned nothing. The node would simply have been unreachable for reasons that look
like a network fault. `manifests/README.md` was JSON under a `.md` name and still
claimed the registry was produced "once Step 3 exists".

**A contradiction in the contract, recorded rather than resolved.** The protocol
promised re-formation as the escape from a blocked election, while requiring
unanimity at n=3 — so the case needing re-formation is exactly the case where it
is impossible. The honest statement is that a three-node cluster cannot survive
the loss of a member. Three resolutions are written down with their costs; picking
one is a policy call, not mine to make silently.

**Not verified, and not claimed.** The signer has never run in a live cluster.
Its unit test proves topology, not that a real deployment held — that part of
DoD (3)/(5) is still `confirm: user`, and it is what gates enabling the unit.
Election remains unbuilt, so the signer has no root to bind to in practice.

## Session part 2 — Step 18: the admin surface

**What landed.** Three admin-geared agents (`cluster-admin`, `registry-keeper`,
`evidence-reviewer`) in `master-node/.opencode/agents/`, a three-layer
`MASTERSPIKE-manifest.json` (raw / wiki / schema, all oriented to
administration), a machine-readable `manifests/admin-surface.json`, and
`configs/admin-surface/` holding the Open WebUI compose file plus the opencode
terminal config. Port 8080 is contracted as `provisioned` /
`not-verifiable-here`. `make wiki-preflight` resolves `master-node/`.

**The one idea worth carrying forward.** The admin page is an LLM application
that reads configuration as input, so it must be reachable by an agent and still
be unable to sign. That is only safe because authority does not flow through this
port: grants are signed at 4203/4210 in the isolated signer process. `sign` sits
in `explicitly_not_granted`, the terminal config denies the state dir and
`signer.sock`, and tests assert all three. This is Step 17's structural isolation
paying off — a policy promise ("admins don't sign") would have been worthless
here, since the surface is exactly where a prompt injection would arrive.

**Three defects, all in the tests rather than the code — and that is the pattern
worth naming.**

1. *The harness lied about the artifact.* `opencode agent list` emits ~12 MB for
   this role; `subprocess(capture_output=True)` truncated it to ~1 MB and dropped
   the role's own agents, which sort last. The test reported `cluster-admin does
   not resolve` and pointed at the agent file. The DoD now says "read via a file,
   never a pipe" — it was load-bearing, not stylistic.
2. *Assertions wider than their intent.* The compose scan rejected the state-dir
   mention in a comment saying it must never be mounted, and the MASTERSPIKE path
   resolver guessed roots by prefix, so `master-node/...` doubled up. Both now
   assert on what the config *does*: non-comment lines, and paths resolved by
   trying both roots.
3. *A test tripping over reassurance.* `test_no_vendor_manifest_points_at_the_
   signer_port` scanned the whole serialised `ports.json` and failed once port
   8080's note explained that authority messages are signed *elsewhere*. The note
   reassuring a reader that the signer is unreachable broke the test whose job is
   keeping it unreachable. It now scans port declarations only, and I confirmed it
   still catches a port actually declared for the signer.

**A self-inflicted incident, recorded because it nearly went unnoticed.** To undo
mutation M19 I ran `git checkout contracts/ports.json`. That reverted the file to
the boolean-era HEAD version and destroyed five ports (4200–4203, 4210) and the
entire `implementation_state` / `runtime` two-axis rework. `make contract-test`
caught it within the same command. The file was restored from the last verified
bundle and every one of the original twelve entries now compares equal to it.
Two lessons are in `MEMORY.md`: the mutation runner must save-and-restore the file
it mutates like all the others (it saved four and skipped this one), and an
uncommitted contract has no `git checkout` safety net — the bundle is the only
copy that survives.

**Mutation report.** M13 (surface granted `sign`), M14 (empty schema layer), M15
(layer re-oriented to `peer`), M16 (raw layer citing a nonexistent file), M17
(wildcard bind), M18 (compose mounts a key directory), M19 (port claimed
`implemented`) — all caught. M20 first reported "survived" because the `sed` anchor
did not match the bullet's actual text, so the file was never edited: the same
false all-clear as M10 earlier in the session. Reapplied as an exact replacement,
it fails as it should. A mutation runner that reports a pass it did not produce
is worse than no runner, so both no-ops are in the log.

**Not verified, and not claimed.** Nothing binds `10.100.0.1:8080` — Open WebUI is
not installed here and no overlay interface exists — so Step 18 (3)'s
reachability stays `DoD: confirm: user`, and the port is `provisioned` rather than
`implemented` because config without a listener is not a page.

**Gates.** `make test-all` (contract 26 + 1 skip, fabric, peer 185 + 1 skip,
master 77), `make lint`, `make validate`, `make contract-test` — all green.
Bundle `dist/sparknet-master-worktree-20261005-172441.tar.gz` built and
extraction-verified, admin artifacts present, master parity resolving to the
bundled peer. Cursor `18` → `19`.

## Session part 3 — Step 19: honest verification, and what it found

**The simulation the DoD asked for.** `tests/test_election_simulation.py`, 15
tests, real Ed25519 keys throughout — a mocked verifier would pass whether or not
the protocol worked, which is the failure the consensus module's own docstring
warns about. Three real nodes vote; the elected master's operational key then
signs a registry entry and only that key authorises it. Refusals cover a forged
third ballot, an unsigned ballot, another member's genuine signature relabelled as
a third voter, an outsider, and a forged vote trying to launder an equivocation.
Re-formation elects a different master under a new election and root nonce. The
2 h window is a constant; an AST walk asserts nothing in the file calls `sleep`,
because grepping the source matched the test's own docstring — a test that cannot
tell a call from a sentence about a call is not evidence.

**It found a hole that mattered, and the hole was old.** `tally()` deduplicated
votes by `node_id` and never called `verify_vote()`. A vote is a network packet,
so anyone could forge a ballot naming a member who had not voted and manufacture
unanimity outright. It survived twenty-four consensus tests because none of them
had ever forged anything: the property was never exercised, so it was never
tested. Verification now runs before counting, unverifiable votes are dropped
**and reported** in `rejected`, because an election that tolerates forgery must
still be able to show that it did. Moving verification to *after* counting also
fails the suite (M23) — the obvious "clean up" of that loop would have reopened it.

**It also caught the contract lying, twice.** `consensus-protocol.md` claimed "a
new nonce means the old master_pubkey stops being recognised". It does not.
`verify_root` sees one root and the root already held; two roots with different
nonces are both internally valid, and nothing inside the old one says it was
replaced. Supersession genuinely lives in the ledger's `root_id` records and
`check_not_reverted`; a peer has to *learn* a re-formed root before the old Master
stops being recognised by it, and **how it learns is undecided**. That second
question is now recorded next to the n=3 one, with the reason it is not a detail:
announcing over a channel the superseded Master also controls lets it suppress the
news of its own replacement, which is the textbook argument for a lease rather
than a broadcast. `identity-schema.json` carried the same overclaim in
`on_master_change`; corrected too.

**A stale comment, found by looking at the wrong thing.** `sparknet-bundle.sh` said
the Master "has no code" — true until Step 15 gave it a surface. More useful than
the fix: what a bundle does *not* contain. The fabric-level `tests/` are not
shipped, so an extracted node cannot run `test_contracts.py` and cannot check
contract drift for itself. Parity is proved at the build host before bundling, not
inside the bundle, and now the script says so where an operator will read it.

**Not verified, and claimed nowhere.** Step 19's page lists seven: live admin-page
reachability, deployed signer containment, election across a real overlay, how a
re-formed root reaches a peer, n=3 survival of a member loss, this host's
WireGuard/nftables/systemd behaviour, and Steps 1–12 being specified rather than
built. The last is the one that keeps this honest: Phase 0 and the role's own
boundary work are done, and the fabric is still not running.

**Where the cursor goes.** `19` → `1`. Not a regression and not a retry: Steps 1–12
are the remaining work, and the plan's own sequencing note already said they
assumed a Master that 13–14 had not yet produced. 617 tests green — 355 fabric,
185 peer, 77 master, 1 skip — with lint, validate, contract-test and
wiki-preflight clean, and `dist/sparknet-master-worktree-20261005-174041.tar.gz`
built and extraction-verified.
